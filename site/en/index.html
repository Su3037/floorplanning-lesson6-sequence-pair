Floorplanning · Lesson 6 / Module 06 / Sequence-Pair

Two lines of letters
lay out a chip

How do two rows of letters decide which of five blocks is on the left, and which is on top?

Floorplanning places rectangular blocks (cores, caches, interfaces) inside a chip’s fixed outline, without overlap and as compactly as possible. A sequence pair records the relative position of every pair of blocks with two permutations, then computes coordinates as longest paths in two constraint graphs. This lesson follows five small blocks step by step: what the method guarantees, and what it does not.

01 / This lesson

What you will be able to do

about 90–120 minutes (four films about 11, lesson 30, lab and challenges 30, Python 30)

  1. State the inputs, outputs and constraints of floorplanning, and explain why searching coordinates directly is awkward and why a representation helps.
  2. Read off the relative position of any two blocks from pos and neg (four cases), and find every problem in an invalid pair.
  3. Turn relations into horizontal and vertical constraint graphs, write each edge’s inequality, and explain why the graphs have no cycle.
  4. Compute every block’s coordinates by longest-path dynamic programming, and explain why the predecessor’s size is added and why the maximum is taken.
  5. Answer complete, overlap-free, fits and compact as four separate questions, telling quantities that never change with the pair from those that do.
  6. Reason about how changing a permutation changes the geometry (including “different encoding, same packing”), and implement validation, graph building, DP and metrics in Python.

Before you start

Reading 2-D coordinates and rectangle areas; knowing what a directed graph is (see Foundations if not). No chip-design background is needed.

What it is not

This is a teaching example with a handful of rectangles, made to show the representation and the constraint reasoning. Real floorplanning also weighs wirelength, timing, power and pins; detailed placement, routing and sign-off are done by dedicated EDA tools.

Where it sits in the course

Lab 06 of the instructor’s learn_floorplanning course (catalogue id module02-05-sequence-pair). The lesson before is B*-tree; the one after is simulated annealing, a random search over sequence pairs.
